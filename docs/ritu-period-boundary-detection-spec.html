Period Boundary Detection Spec

Governs how the app decides where one period ends and the next begins, given daily flow logs that can include gaps, missed days, or no logging at all. Directly affects Menstrual-phase duration (P) and cycle length (C) used throughout the Classification and Phase-Range specs.

1. Overview

Ritu's daily Log screen asks "Any flow today?" with options None / Spotting / Light / Medium / Heavy. Period start and end are derived from this daily input. Because flow is often not continuous — spotting gaps, days with no bleeding mid-period, or days the user simply doesn't open the app — a naive "period ends the moment a non-flow day appears" rule can silently corrupt the data feeding classification.

2. The Core Problem — Spotting Gaps

A single logged gap day can be misread as the period ending, and the next bleeding day misread as a brand new period starting. This creates a fake, very short "cycle" between the two.

DayShe logsNaive interpretation
Day 1SpottingPeriod started
Day 2NoneWrong — reads as period ended
Day 3NoneStill reads as ended
Day 4MediumWrong — reads as a new period starting
Why this matters: the false 3-day gap becomes a fake "cycle length" fed into the Classification Spec's Mean Absolute Deviation calculation. Since MAD averages deviations across the last 6 cycles, one nonsensical short cycle skews the result — potentially misclassifying a user with a normal spotting pattern as more irregular than she actually is.

3. Rule 1 — Grace-Gap Threshold

Decision: a period does not close on the first "None" day. It closes only after 2 consecutive "None" days in a row, or automatically at day 10 since period start — whichever comes first.

ThresholdValueWhy
Consecutive "None" days to close21 day is indistinguishable from a normal spotting gap (too short, doesn't fix the problem). 3+ days risks merging a genuinely new period into the old one, especially for shorter-cycle users.
Hard cap since period start10 daysSafety net — prevents an edge case where ongoing light/irregular spotting could stretch the "still the same period" window indefinitely.

Re-checking the earlier example with this rule applied:

DayShe logsCorrect interpretation
Day 1SpottingPeriod started
Day 2None1 consecutive None — not enough to close yet
Day 3None2 consecutive None — would close here, but...
Day 4MediumCorrect — bleeding resumed before the close condition finished evaluating; treated as the same period continuing
Implementation detail: the 2-day close condition should only finalize (lock in the period-end date) once Day 3's "None" is confirmed with no new flow logged on Day 3 itself. If flow appears on what would have been the 2nd consecutive None day, the streak resets to 0 and the period stays open.

4. Rule 2 — Silence Is Not "None"

The grace-gap counter in Rule 1 only increments on an explicit "None" tap. It must never increment because the user simply didn't open the app that day.

Day 2 scenarioCounts toward the 2-day close threshold?
She taps "None"Yes — a real signal she checked and there's no flow
She doesn't open the app at allNo — silence isn't evidence of anything
Why this distinction matters: if a skipped day counted the same as "None," a user who simply forgets to log for a day or two could have her period wrongly closed out early — a worse outcome than the original spotting-gap bug, since now it penalizes inactivity rather than fixing a data problem.

5. Rule 3 — Assumed Duration Fallback

If she logs Day 1 and then doesn't open the app at all (no logging of any kind, not even "None"), the hero card does not wait or freeze. It continues to advance day-by-day using calendar math, exactly as an actively-logging user would see, backed by an assumed period duration rather than a confirmed one.

SituationP used
She has a personal average period length from historyHer own average P
No personal average yet, but she picked a duration bucket at onboarding ("4–5 days," etc.)The bucket she selected
No data at allA sensible default (e.g. 5 days)

Example: assumed P = 5 days, she logs Day 1 and goes silent. Hero card shows Menstrual through Day 5, then automatically switches to Follicular on Day 6 — using the assumed value, since there's nothing else to go on.

This is provisional, not final. If she later logs something that reveals her period actually ran longer or shorter than the assumed P, the hero card displayed the wrong phase in the interim — an acceptable tradeoff, since silence must default to something. But the true duration used for her classification math must come from what she eventually confirms, never from the assumed value shown while she was inactive. See Section 6 for how this gets resolved.

6. Rule 4 — Home Check-In Nudge

Rather than silently keep guessing indefinitely, the app asks once. This keeps with an existing project principle — "Honest about estimated data" — extended to period-duration estimates, not just phase estimates.

6.1 Why Home, not the Log screen

The Log screen already asks "Any flow today?" daily — adding a second, similar question there would be redundant. More importantly, the entire scenario is that she has stopped opening Log; placing the question there means it would never reach her. Home is the surface she is still likely to open even after several days of no logging — the same reasoning behind the existing "Speed up your patterns" nudge card, which also lives on Home for a lapsed-logging moment.

6.2 Trigger condition

Shown once, the first time she opens the app after the assumed P boundary has passed with no new flow log (not even "None") since Day 1.

6.3 Card content and behavior

ElementDetail
PlacementDismissible card on Home, directly beneath the hero card
Headline"Still on your period?"
Body copy"It's been a few days since your last log — just checking so Ritu can keep your pattern accurate."
Button — "Still going"Dismisses the card. Hero card keeps counting Menstrual using the assumed P. No repeat ask that day.
Button — "It ended"Opens the follow-up screen (Section 7) to confirm the actual end date.
Dismiss (X)Same effect as "Still going" — falls back to assumed P, asks again only at the next natural trigger point, never repeatedly the same day.
Tone stays calm, no urgency language, no red — consistent with the project's "no alarm, no diagnosis" principle. This is a light, one-time nudge, not a hard gate blocking her from using the rest of the app.

7. Follow-Up Screen — Confirm Period End

Shown only after she taps "It ended" on the Home nudge.

ElementDetail
Headline"When did it end?"
Subhead"Approximate is fine — you can always adjust later" (same reassurance phrasing used at onboarding, Screen 4)
Quick options"Yesterday," "2 days ago," "Today" — each showing the actual calendar date beneath the label, since most answers are recent and a full calendar would be overkill
Fallback"Pick a different date" — opens the same calendar picker style already used in onboarding, for the less common case of a longer gap
Confirm buttonLocks in the selected date as her true period end date
No "Skip" hereUnlike the Home nudge, once she's committed to "It ended," this is a short, low-friction confirm rather than something to abandon halfway
Effect once confirmed: the confirmed date replaces the assumed P for that cycle. The hero card corrects retroactively if needed (e.g. any days shown as Menstrual past the real end date get reclassified into Follicular on next render), and the real, confirmed duration — not the assumed one — is what feeds the Classification Spec's cycle-length calculation going forward.

8. Full Flow Summary

StepWhat happens
1Period starts — logged via an explicit flow entry (any option other than None triggers Day 1)
2Each subsequent day, flow is logged normally, including "None" days for a genuine gap
3Period closes automatically after 2 consecutive explicit "None" days, or at day 10, whichever comes first (Rule 1)
4If she stops logging entirely, the hero card keeps advancing using an assumed P (Rule 3) — this never happens due to silence alone, only due to actual "None" taps (Rule 2)
5Once the assumed P boundary passes with no confirmation, Home shows the one-time "Still on your period?" nudge (Rule 4)
6If she confirms it ended, the follow-up screen (Section 7) captures the real end date, correcting the data used for classification
